03 — Component schedule

What each block actually is.

REF C
Backbone

Factorised spatiotemporal attention

Attention runs spatially across the 23 entities, then temporally across time — d=256, 4 layers, 8 heads. The standard structure for multi-agent trajectories.

REF C
Generative head

Conditional diffusion (DDPM)

ε-prediction, linear β-schedule, ~100 reverse steps. Returns a distribution of futures — sample K and pick, not one deterministic guess.

REF D
Control

Waypoint + classifier-free guidance

The drawn arrow becomes a per-entity waypoint; trained under CFG dropout so weight w tunes how strictly the arrow is honoured — suggestion to instruction.

REF E
Constraints

Deterministic physics pass

Seam anchoring, per-frame max-speed clip, exponential smoothing, on-pitch clamp w/ 2 m margin, pairwise repulsion. 0.0% off-pitch.

REF —
Evaluation

Honest metrics

minADE / minFDE, diversity, off-pitch rate, arrow-honour error under a CFG sweep — reported per checkpoint against a constant-velocity floor.

REF A
Data plane

Provider-agnostic ingest

Open Metrica format live today; Hawk-Eye / Sportec / Stats Perform / SkillCorner adapters scoped per engagement. Source token + validity mask handle mixed feeds.

04 — Design rationale

Why this isn't a prompt wrapper.

The difference is the unit of work. A wrapper describes a scene in tokens and hopes the pixels comply. We solve for the physical state of every body on the pitch first, then render it.

GENERIC Text-to-video wrapper

  • Players appear and vanish; counts drift mid-clip
  • No control over who goes where — you negotiate with a prompt
  • Physics is whatever the pixels felt like
  • Nothing to measure; nothing to audit

GENTAC Trajectory-first model

  • Fixed 22 players + ball, coherent for the whole horizon
  • Per-entity waypoint control with a guidance dial
  • Speed, bounds and collisions enforced deterministically
  • Every output scored against a constant-velocity baseline
