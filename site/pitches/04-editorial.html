A position paperB START UP · 2026On the counterfactual in football

A new primitive
for the game: the alternative.

Football has always had two media for a decision — the replay, which shows what happened, and the tactics board, which gestures at what should have. It has never had a medium for the alternative actually playing out. We built one.

Consider the most common sentence in the history of the sport: he should have passed. It is spoken in living rooms, dugouts and committee chambers a million times a weekend, and it has always ended the same way — in disagreement, because there is no way to see the other version. The argument cannot be settled, only repeated.

This is not for lack of data. Modern football is among the most instrumented activities on earth: every player's position is captured many times a second, by optical systems the major leagues already license. We can describe what happened with extraordinary precision. What we have never been able to do is render what didn't.

The replay is a recording; it cannot deviate from the truth. The tactics board can deviate, but only as a doodle on a frozen still — drag one arrow and the other twenty-one players stay rooted to the spot, the pass never travels, the defence never reacts. Between a recording that cannot change and a drawing that cannot move, the alternative has had no home.

GenTac is the argument that it should — and the system that gives it one.

§ I

The counterfactual needed a medium.

A medium is more than an effect. It is a stable, repeatable way of producing a class of thing — the way the slow-motion replay is a medium, not a trick. For the alternative to become a medium it must satisfy three conditions, all at once: it must be plausible (players move as players actually move), controllable (you can specify the one change you want to test), and legible (the result can be watched, scored and trusted).

Each of those is a hard problem on its own, and the reason no tactics board has ever crossed the gap. Plausibility requires modelling all the bodies on the pitch as one interacting system. Control requires turning a human gesture into a precise instruction. Legibility requires the output to be a real video, not a diagram. The premise of this paper is that those three conditions are now jointly achievable.

§ II

The model, stated plainly.

The input is one drawn arrow: a player, and the path you wish they had taken. The system reads the four seconds of real tracking leading into the moment, treats your arrow as a waypoint, and asks a generative model what the rest of the pitch does in response.

That model is a conditional diffusion model over the joint trajectory of all twenty-three entities — twenty-two players and the ball — faithful to the published GenTac research rather than a thin wrapper around a chatbot. A guidance control decides how strictly the model must honour your arrow, from gentle suggestion to firm instruction. A deterministic physics pass then enforces what is real: a maximum running speed, the boundaries of the pitch, the fact that two bodies cannot occupy one point. The result is not the model's best single guess but a distribution of plausible alternatives, any of which can be rendered to video.

The league already owns the rights, the data and the broadcast. The instrument that turns those three into a new category of footage should sit with the league as well.

§ III

Why it belongs to the league.

A single club deploying this is a point solution — interesting internally, invisible to everyone else. A league deploying it is a new product line that compounds across every match, every season and every supporter. And uniquely, the league is the one party that already holds all three inputs the medium requires.

From one engine, four surfaces follow. Broadcast: a graphics layer for the world feed during the analysis window, licensed to rights-holders, owned by the competition. Supporters: a "what-if" surface in the official app, where engagement becomes inventory. Competition integrity: a calm, repeatable way for the technical desk to study decisive moments. Member clubs: one standardised tool issued to every analysis department, with no data leaving the building. The same brain; four shapes.
