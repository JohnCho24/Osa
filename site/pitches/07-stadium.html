01

It starts with what actually happened.

GenTac reads the match's own optical tracking — the real positions of all 22 players and the ball, the four seconds leading into the decision everyone's still arguing about.

02

Then someone draws the other choice.

A coach, an analyst, a fan: tap the player, drag the run they should have made. A dial decides how strictly the model honours it. One gesture is the whole interface.

03

The whole pitch comes alive.

A diffusion model rolls every player forward off that single arrow. Teammates adjust, defenders react, the ball travels at a believable speed — kept on the pitch and out of each other by a physics pass.

04

And it ends on the screen.

The alternative renders to broadcast-quality video — handed to the studio feed, the league app, or the review desk. Not a diagram of the second ending. The second ending itself.

The replay shows what happened. The board argues what should have. Only this lets you watch it.
